# Open-aspect sentiment: how it is trained and evaluated

**Retrieval date:** 2026-11-03
**Scope:** Research for [#177](https://github.com/neoyipeng2018/nlp-wayfinder/issues/177) (map [#176](https://github.com/neoyipeng2018/nlp-wayfinder/issues/176)). It feeds [#179](https://github.com/neoyipeng2018/nlp-wayfinder/issues/179), which decides where the open-aspect training strings come from and how they are labelled.
**Sources:** Papers (read from the PDFs), official repos, dataset cards and Kaggle metadata. "Unverified" marks anything not confirmed from a primary source.

## Bottom line for #179

1. **Positive strings should come from the passage, generated by an LLM and checked by humans.** Every recent pipeline that scales past a fixed taxonomy does this. VAST draws topics from the text and lets annotators correct and add to them. Arctic-ABSA and IterD have an LLM propose aspects. A fixed taxonomy (FiQA, SentiHood, SemEval categories) caps the strings the model will ever see, and #176 rules that out.
2. **"Insufficient evidence" strings should be borrowed from other passages, then filtered by a judge.** This is the most common published recipe:
   - VAST converts about 50% of its clean examples into neutral ones by giving the comment a topic from elsewhere in the dataset. It rejects any borrowed topic that overlaps lexically with the original topic or with the annotator topics. That gives 3,984 of 23,525 examples.
   - Arctic-ABSA adds 2 to 4 aspects (uniform) taken from the dataset-wide aspect pool to 25% of its samples. An LLM judge then removes any borrowed aspect that the text mentions *implicitly*. The paper names those as false negatives under naive sampling.
   - The judge step matters for us. In news, a borrowed string like "pricing power" is often implicitly covered, and the label should be a polarity, not insufficient evidence.
3. **Each passage should carry several strings, including some with *different* polarity from the passage overall.** Two results support this:
   - MAMS keeps only sentences with at least two aspects of different polarity, and ARTS shows models trained on it are more robust to aspect confounders.
   - ARTS-style counterfactual augmentation (reverse the non-target aspect, add distractor aspects) gives a larger gain still, a robust-accuracy lift of at least 11.87 points.
   Without such examples, the model can score well by predicting document sentiment.
4. **The unseen strings in the test should be held out by string *cluster*, not by exact string.** VAST keeps zero-shot topics and their documents out of training, and it also checks how many test topics are near-duplicates of training topics (GloVe cosine ≥ 0.9). Our sealed test should report three slices: seen strings, unseen-but-lexically-close strings, and unseen-and-distant strings. It should also report two baselines: aspect-only and passage-only (document sentiment).
5. **There are no finance datasets with company + free-text aspect + sentiment that we may train on for an Apache-2.0 release.** The few finance sets are eval-only at best (table below). The training strings must come from our own clean-core passages with GPT-6.1 labels.

## Q1. How training aspect strings are sourced

| Work | String source | "None"/insufficient source | Mix / counts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **VAST** (Allaway & McKeown, EMNLP 2020) | Noun phrases heuristically taken from the subject or object of the main verb, or NYT article categories when none are found. Annotators correct these and list more topics. | A topic borrowed from another comment, with a lexical-overlap check against the original and annotator topics. | Each Heur/Corr entry has a 0.5 chance of being converted. 3,984 neutral of 23,525 examples; 5,634 topics; median 4 unique topics per comment; median 1 example per topic. [§3.1–3.2, Tables 3–4](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.717.pdf) |
| **Arctic-ABSA** (Liskowski & Jankowski, Snowflake, arXiv 2601.03940, Jan 2026) | Upside-down generation: categories (manual + LLM) → LLM-generated aspects and keywords → sampled aspect–sentiment pairs → LLM writes the text in a sampled persona style. | "Unknown" aspects drawn from all aspects in the dataset minus the sample's own, then checked by an LLM judge that the aspect is not explicitly *or implicitly* mentioned. | Applied to 25% of samples; 2–4 unknown aspects per augmented sample (uniform). The seed corpus has 1,832 aspects and 3,463 keywords. [§4.2–4.5, Alg. 1, Table 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.03940) |
| **IterD** (arXiv 2407.00341) | The LLM extracts and deduplicates domain aspects from unlabelled text, then generates labelled data from that set iteratively. | n/a | Not read in full. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00341) |
| **SentiHood** (Saeidi et al., COLING 2016) / **Sun et al.** (NAACL 2019) | Fixed list of 11 aspects; papers use the top 4 (price, safety, transit-location, general). | Full cross product of target × aspect; any pair with no opinion is labelled "None". | Fixed taxonomy, so it is unbalanced toward None. [SentiHood](https://aclanthology.org/C16-1146.pdf), [Sun et al.](https://aclanthology.org/N19-1035.pdf) |
| **FiQA 2018 Task 1** | A predefined list of aspect classes in a two-level form such as "Corporate/Strategy"; continuous score in [-1, 1]. | None | 1,173 items (822/117/234 in FLUE). [FiQA site](https://sites.google.com/view/fiqa/home), [FLUE Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.00083) |
| **MAMS** (Jiang et al., EMNLP 2019) | Terms and categories from SemEval-style annotation of Citysearch reviews. | n/a | Keeps only sentences with ≥2 aspects of *different* polarity. [paper](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1654.pdf) |

What this means for us:
- A per-passage mix in the Arctic/VAST style would be about 2–4 positive strings proposed from the passage (including one paraphrase of a Stage 1 aspect).
- On about 25–50% of passages, it would add 1–4 strings borrowed from other passages, after an LLM or GPT-6.1 judge pass for implicit coverage.
- The five fixed strings stay as they are.
- These proportions are borrowed from the literature, not tuned. Nobody publishes an ablation of the mix ratio.

## Q2. How unseen-aspect generalisation is evaluated

- **VAST zero-shot vs few-shot topics.** The zero-shot dev/test topics *and their documents* never appear in training; the few-shot topics do. The test set has 600 zero-shot and 159 few-shot topics. BERT-joint scores macro-F1 0.661 on zero-shot and 0.646 on few-shot; TGA Net scores 0.666 and 0.663. So the seen/unseen gap is about zero once the model is conditioned on the topic. The large gap is between *lexically similar* and *dissimilar* unseen topics: LexSimTopics (16% of the zero-shot test) score 0.70–0.72 F1, which is 0.04–0.06 above the rest. The *hardest* slice is documents carrying different stances for different topics ("mlS"): BERT-joint accuracy is 0.541 on that slice versus 0.713 elsewhere. [Tables 5, 7](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.717.pdf)
- **Seoh et al. (EMNLP 2021), "Open aspect target sentiment classification".** They train on explicit aspect terms and test on aspect *categories* (implicit, a different distribution). The NLI/prompt reformulation reaches about 77% versus about 65% for the no-prompt baseline, with only 16 training reviews. So the way the aspect is fed to the model matters most in low-data settings. [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.509.pdf)
- **ARTS (Xing et al., EMNLP 2020).** This is the robustness metric (ARS), not an unseen split. Accuracy on the original SemEval-14 test against ARTS: BERT 83.0 → 54.8 (restaurant), BERT-PT 86.7 → 59.3. The average over nine models drops by about 46–48 points. [Table 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.07964)
- **Domain zero-shot ABSA** (Shu et al., CORN, arXiv 2202.01924) holds out *domains*, not aspect strings, so it is less relevant here.

Typical gaps: seen and unseen topics differ by about 0–2 F1 when the topics are truly disjoint and many (VAST). Lexical distance costs 4–6 F1. Aspect confounding costs 20–50 accuracy points. Treat confounding as the main risk, not novelty of the string.

## Q4. Known failure modes and mitigations

1. **Ignoring the aspect, falling back to document sentiment.**
   - Evidence: in ARTS, BERT-Sent, a model with no aspect input, gets a respectable original accuracy (80.6 restaurant) but falls to 10.9 on ARTS. ABSA models fall *toward* it: on RevNon, CapsBERT drops 39 points.
   - Mitigations:
     - (a) Train on mixed-polarity passages (MAMS).
     - (b) Counterfactual augmentation on the training data (ARTS §5.4: adversarial training beat MAMS training on restaurants).
     - (c) Multiple strings per passage with different labels (VAST mlS).
   - For us: report a **passage-only baseline** (OV prediction copied to every string) and the share of passages where the string labels disagree.
2. **Aspect-string leakage, where the label is predictable from the string alone.**
   - Example: a string like "tariffs" or "layoffs" is almost always negative. In VAST, the cluster-majority baseline (CMaj) gets 0.286 zero-shot F1, a floor for topic priors.
   - Mitigations:
     - An aspect-only baseline, in the spirit of hypothesis-only NLI baselines ([Poliak et al., *SEM 2018](https://aclanthology.org/S18-2023/)).
     - Hold out by topic *and* document (VAST assigns documents to partitions).
     - Check lexical neighbours between train and test strings (VAST's LexSim threshold).
     - Balance polarity within frequent strings where possible.
3. **False "insufficient" negatives.** A borrowed string can be implicitly addressed in the passage. Arctic-ABSA names this risk and adds an LLM-judge check. For #179, the GPT-6.1 label pass should *label* borrowed strings rather than assume insufficient evidence.
4. **Paraphrase sensitivity.**
   - No primary source found that benchmarks paraphrased aspect/target strings directly for ABSA. The nearest evidence is:
     - VAST's 4–6 F1 gap between lexically close and distant unseen topics.
     - General prompt-format sensitivity of up to 76 accuracy points for LLaMA-2-13B few-shot ([Sclar et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324)), which is about prompt format, not fine-tuned classifiers.
   - Mitigations: train with several phrasings per concept (VAST annotators' topic variants; Arctic's keywords per aspect). Evaluate a **paraphrase-consistency** slice: the same passage with 2–3 rewordings of one string, scored on label agreement.
5. **Neutral-boundary inconsistency.** Arctic-ABSA reports that the neutral class is "inconsistently annotated across datasets" and that mixing sources hurt SemEval scores. This is one more reason not to mix third-party labels into our GPT-6.1 training set.

## Gaps

- No paper ablates the positive : borrowed-negative ratio or the number of strings per passage. The 25% / 2–4 (Arctic) and 0.5 (VAST) figures are design choices, not optima.
- No finance-specific open-aspect benchmark exists, so our sealed test will be the first measurement.
- The MAMS and SentiHood sizes and licences were not confirmed from the release files.

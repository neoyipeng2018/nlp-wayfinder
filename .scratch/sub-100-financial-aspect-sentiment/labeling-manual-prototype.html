Draft manual

Use this order

  1. Check the target family.
  2. Check the aspect.
  3. Find evidence for this target and this aspect.
  4. Resolve negation, source, and time.
  5. Apply the family label set.
1. Target and aspect checks

2. Evidence and aspect boundary
  • Use only the supplied passage.
  • Use evidence about the supplied target.
  • Use the most specific aspect that the claim supports.
  • For a company, the specific subject aspect takes priority over the general outlook aspect.
  • Future time alone does not make a claim an outlook claim.
  • Do not copy one claim to a wider aspect.
  • Risk needs an explicit exposure, uncertainty, weakness, or ability to absorb a shock.

Use insufficient evidence when the pair is valid but the passage does not support it.

3. Company labels
  • Positive: The evidence is favorable for the company and the supplied aspect.
  • Negative: The evidence is unfavorable for the company and the supplied aspect.
  • Neutral: The passage states stability, no material effect, or an explicit net result with no favorable or unfavorable direction.
  • Insufficient evidence: The evidence is absent, unclear, or about another aspect.
4. Macro-indicator labels
  • Increase: The indicator or pressure on it moves up.
  • Decrease: The indicator or pressure on it moves down.
  • Unchanged: The passage states stability or no material movement.
  • Insufficient evidence: The direction is absent or unclear.

Use the movement of the stated measure. Distinguish a level from its rate of change. Do not use whether the movement is good or bad. Do not change the label because the result differs from a forecast.

5. Mixed evidence
  1. Use an explicit overall result when the passage gives one.
  2. Use the relevant aggregate when subparts move in different directions.
  3. Use neutral or unchanged only when the passage supports no net change or no material effect.
  4. Use insufficient evidence when material claims conflict and the passage gives no resolution or basis to combine them.
6. Negation and time
  • Resolve what the negative word changes. “Did not fall” does not always mean “rose.”
  • Match the evidence to its stated time.
  • Do not use a past result as an outlook label.
  • Future time can support a specific company aspect when that subject is the focus.
7. Source, sarcasm, and other targets
  • A clearly attributed claim is evidence. Keep the speaker and any denial clear.
  • Do not give a claim more weight only because of the speaker's role.
  • If the passage rejects a reported claim, do not treat the claim as fact.
  • Use sarcasm only when words and context make the intended meaning clear. Otherwise, use insufficient evidence.
  • Do not transfer a result from one target to another target.
  • Use a cross-target effect only when the passage states the effect on the supplied target.
